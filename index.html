01The central proposition

What makes us who we are?

We are shaped by everything we encounter: our environment, experiences, memories, relationships, beliefs and decisions.

Over time, these elements influence how we perceive ourselves and the world around us.

But as new experiences emerge, existing understandings can change. What once defined us may no longer represent us.

This creates a continuous process of becoming and unbecoming.

FormationAn identity takes shape through biology, environment, conditioning and experience.
AccumulationExperiences, memories, beliefs and attachments knot into something dense.
ContradictionWhat we believe meets what we now know. The structure shakes.
DissolutionIdentities, assumptions and attachments lose their hold.
ReconstructionA new understanding appears. The process begins again.

06About the book

An inquiry, not a guide.

The Art of Becoming and Unbecoming asks how a person is assembled, and what it means that the assembly never stops.

It draws on philosophical inquiry, the study of human behaviour, perception and conceptual scientific thinking to follow a single question through many rooms: if identity, memory, belief and attachment are all in motion, what is the thing that notices the motion?

It offers no method and promises no arrival. It is written for readers who would rather understand the process than be handed a conclusion about it.
